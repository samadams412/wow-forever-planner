Every new WoW Forever zone got a Deep Dive slide and a hype cycle this week. Zephras Isle got both, plus something Mount Hyjal and the Riverglades didn't: a receipt. It's the starting zone for the Skyborne, Forever's new playable race, and it's easily the most striking-looking of the new zones revealed so far -- mist-wrapped watchtowers, standing stones, coastline that looks pulled straight out of Eversong Woods. It's also, as of this week, bundled behind a $29.99 purchase, which turned what should have been the reveal's easiest crowd-pleaser into its most contested one.

## A pact with the wind

The pitch, per community coverage of the Deep Dive panel: long before Forever's present day, a band of Highborne refugees -- the same reckless-with-magic night elf caste history blames for helping cause the Sundering -- landed on an island already inhabited by air elementals. Instead of fighting the local spirits, they cut a deal. That pact produced the Skyborne: elves who traded away some of their old arcane inheritance for an elemental one, and have spent the centuries since finding out what that trade actually cost them.

[Warcraft Tavern's coverage](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/forever/news/all-new-zones-areas-in-wow-forever-revealed-at-blizzcon/) says the developers cited Eversong Woods -- the Blood Elves' TBC starting zone -- as a visual touchstone, and going by Blizzard's own screenshots, that holds up: crimson-leaved trees, pale stone towers, mist that never quite burns off. It reads less like Darnassus-blue Night Elf architecture and more like its wind-swept, autumnal cousin, which suits a people descended from Highborne stock but reshaped by a very different bargain.

## A race split down the middle

Blizzard's own framing calls the Skyborne "noble yet divided," and that's not just flavor text -- it's built into the race itself. Skyborne characters split into two named, faction-flavored branches rather than sharing one kit: Horde-aligned characters play **Windshaper Skyborne**, Alliance-aligned characters play **High Order Skyborne**. Same starting zone, same base kit, but the class list and one racial ability differ per side, and the difference tracks the zone's central conflict almost too neatly.

Windshaper Skyborne can train as a Shaman but not a Mage; High Order Skyborne is the reverse. Their one unique racial ability splits the same way: Windshapers get Skysight, which "draws power from a convergence of elements" for a temporary speed buff, while High Order gets Read Ley Line instead, which taps a nearby ley line -- arcane infrastructure, not elemental -- for a burst of health and mana regen. Everything else in the kit is shared between them: Walk on Air (a controlled glide), Wind Blessed (a small haste buff), and Elemental Insight (bonus damage to elementals). One ability differs per side, and it happens to be the one that encodes "kept faith with the old pact" versus "started leaning on something else" -- confirmed from our own beta VOD footage, not datamined guesswork.

## Level 1 to 12, and then what

Zephras Isle is confirmed as a 1-12 starting zone -- short by Classic standards, closer in scope to the Blood Elf or Draenei starting islands from The Burning Crusade than to something like Elwynn Forest's long, meandering questline. That tracks with everything else said about the race: a self-contained origin story that carries Skyborne characters through their first dozen levels before handing them off to the wider world.

What isn't confirmed yet is where that handoff goes. Blood Elves and Draenei both funnel into their home continent's zones once their starting islands end; nothing announced so far says whether Windshaper Skyborne route toward Kalimdor's Horde zones, High Order Skyborne toward the Eastern Kingdoms' Alliance zones, or whether Zephras Isle connects directly to one of Forever's other three new zones -- Mount Hyjal, the Riverglades, or Shen'Dralas. Worth watching once beta opens on September 17 and people actually walk the exit.

## The zone you have to buy

Here's the part that actually dominated the conversation this week: the Skyborne race, and by extension Zephras Isle itself, isn't included with a base WoW subscription. It ships in three purchasable tiers, [as Wowhead and others reported](https://www.wowhead.com/forever/news/skyborne-race-costs-29-99-to-unlock-for-warcraft-forever-382826) within a day of the reveal. The **Skyborne Heroic Pack** ($29.99) gets you the race, the zone, a transmog set, the Cerulean Prideclaw mount, and a Windwell toy usable in both Midnight and Forever. The **Skyborne Epic Pack** ($59.99) adds 30 days of game time, more transmog and mount options, the original Collector's Edition pets, two Invite-a-Friend codes, and beta access. The **Warcraft Forever Collection** ($79.99) stacks everything from Epic on top of Warcraft 3: Reforged and its new Forsaken Kingdom campaign.

To be clear about what that means: there's currently no announced way to unlock the Skyborne, or set foot in Zephras Isle, without paying above whatever you're already paying for a WoW subscription. That's a genuinely new thing for Classic-side WoW, which has generally treated race and class access as something a subscription already covers -- and it's why the reaction to an otherwise well-received reveal soured fast. "Cash elves" is the label that stuck, and it stuck within a day.

The counterargument going around is straightforward: don't want it, don't buy it, everyone else's Forever experience is unaffected. That's true as far as it goes. It just means Zephras Isle -- easily the best-looking zone shown off this week -- is also the one a chunk of the beta population may never actually set foot in.
