The WoW: Forever dev team pulled back the curtain again this week. Host Josh Corbett (@countdowntoclassic) sat down with Senior Game Designers Josh "Aggrend" Greenfield and Aidan "Zirene" Moon, Principal Designer Kris Zierhut, and special guest Sodapoppin for episode two of the WoW: Forever Podcast — a nearly hour-long, unscripted walkthrough of class design philosophy, spec by spec. If you don't have time to watch the whole thing, here's everything that matters.

**Correction from the video description:** only one new battleground and one new dungeon are going live this week (not the two dungeons mentioned early in the episode) — see the note on dungeon names below.

## How the team thinks about rotations and the hybrid tax

Before diving class by class, Kris Zierhut laid out the guiding philosophy: every spec starts from fantasy, not button count. "What is that class about? What is that spec about? How do they want to play?" The team deliberately keeps core, moment-to-moment rotations — anything under about 10 seconds — to **three or four buttons**. The goal isn't a low-effort rotation; it's leaving room. Cooldowns, procs, and utility only feel good when your global cooldown isn't already jammed full of your core rotation.

That design also means white damage (your basic melee swings) has to matter again. Zierhut pointed to early WoW expansions as a cautionary tale: as yellow (ability) damage crept up and white damage mattered less, "the game became a very different game." Forever is deliberately pulling back toward white damage mattering.

On the controversial **hybrid tax**: Zierhut said flatly he doesn't believe the old approach worked — give hybrids less damage but more unique utility, then watch players complain until damage gets buffed anyway, ending with hybrids doing it all. Forever's approach instead: hybrid *damage and healing output* stays competitive, but hybrids are missing some of the unique buffs and tools that pure-DPS classes (hunter, mage, rogue, warlock) get. The team landed on roughly **5% less damage** for hybrid DPS specs as the practical cost of being able to fill more than one role.

Every class and spec is also being tuned against a shared **damage budget** — the team wants boss fights to take about as long as they did in 2004–2005, so any buff to one class or spec has to come from somewhere else. Early in the beta, the team found *every single spec* was overperforming once talents were factored in, which is being corrected across the board, not just for any one class.

## Class-by-class highlights

### Druid
- **Wrath got a 50% base-damage buff** — but that number looks bigger than it is. It's part of a broader rework where caster gear now carries spell power all the way down to level 10, so leveling casters actually care about their drops the way a rogue or warrior does. Wrath's damage had been nerfed hard early on (there was a scrapped experiment to make it a free, wand-like spell), so the 50% buff mostly restores it rather than making it overtuned. Expect druid damage to get nudged back down elsewhere to stay inside the damage budget.
- **Power shifting is gone**, replaced by a new talent: **Shifting Power**. It costs the same mana as shifting into cat form, grants 40 energy, and has a 16-second cooldown (down to 8 seconds with talents) — replacing Tiger's Fury in the tree. The team wanted to keep the *feel* of power shifting (burning mana for energy, caring about intellect again) without the old mechanic's unintuitive, "why does becoming human and then a cat again refill my energy" logic.
- Feral druids (and hybrids generally) are intentionally being kept weak healers — the team wants hybrid DPS specs to need a dedicated healer, not solo-sustain through fights.

### Warrior
- **Rage from taking damage is currently bugged low** — a known issue, fix targeted for next week's build (not this week's).
- **Rage from critical strikes is returning** — not a flat double, but a **75% increase**, reintroduced because players argued the swingy, high-variance rage bar is core to the warrior fantasy. Designers acknowledged this change happened fast, before the "call to arms" from the community even picked up on the problem.
- Warrior's core identity: **king of sustained cleave/AoE damage** — no other class, the designers said, can sustain whirlwind-style multi-target damage across a whole fight the way a warrior can.
- **Blood Thrill**'s proc chance was buffed this patch. Faster weapons proc it more often for less damage; slower weapons proc it less often for more — a deliberate choice point for arms warriors.
- **Slam** is currently a cooldown rather than a rotational button, which the designers admit isn't where they want it long-term — it's there mainly because of rage economy constraints, and may change as the team sees how rage economy shakes out.
- **Recklessness stays a 30-minute cooldown.** The team tried shortening it (and Shield Wall/Retaliation) to "every boss" availability during testing and found damage numbers blew way past 2005 levels — recklessness combined with crit-generated rage is one of the strongest personal cooldowns in the game.

### Paladin
- **Lay on Hands cooldown cut from 1 hour to 20 minutes** — an hour was "just excessive"; 20 minutes still forces a real decision about when to use it, and it costs your entire mana bar on top of the long cooldown.
- **Twist of Light / seal twisting** stays as designed — the team called the "auto-attack bot" reactive playstyle of early paladins deliberate, not an accident, and doesn't want to abandon that design space. They're open to a Holy Shock-focused build as a legitimate PvE alternative for players who dislike seal twisting, and floated (on-air, as a "what if") a possible bonus for twisting within a tight window before your swing timer — to reward the old-school execution without requiring it.
- **Champion of the Light nerfed**: the intellect-to-spell-power conversion drops from 100% to 60%, and it no longer boosts healing power at all — a direct response to community concern that holy paladins would be forced into retribution's talent tree to stay competitive.

### Rogue
- Subtlety is getting broader weapon support — ambush, hemorrhage, and rupture-style finishers so subtlety doesn't become a pure-dagger-only spec.
- **A new 31-point subtlety capstone is coming: Death Mark** (ported from the "Titan Reforge" Chinese realm). You mark a target, accumulate damage dealt to it over 6 seconds, then it bursts — replacing the current Thousand Cuts talent. In PvP, marking a target telegraphs the burst, giving the target a window to react or peel away.
- No current plans to add extra energy regeneration levers — the designers said rogue damage scales out of control fast once you add energy-regen talents (one early build required cutting Sinister Strike numbers roughly in half to compensate). The payoff: **PvE rogues are intended to be the highest single-target damage class in the game**, standstill-fight conditions permitting.

### Shaman
- **Totem twisting is removed** — like power shifting, the team called it unintended, non-intuitive emergent gameplay that most guilds never even discovered.
- The team is actively discussing moving some raid-stacking buffs (Leader of the Pack, the moonkin/boomkin crit aura) from **party-wide to raid-wide**, specifically to stop raid comps from needing "four shamans and four druids" to cover buff uptime. Nothing confirmed — still in the idea phase.
- Enhancement shaman are deliberately being built to care about spell power on weapons, via Maelstrom Weapon-powered Lightning Bolt — part of a wider design goal of making different stats matter differently to different specs (a deliberate reversal of Cataclysm-era "every stat works for everyone" itemization). Chain Lightning and healing spells were intentionally left off the Maelstrom Weapon proc list — Chain Lightning specifically to protect warrior's cleave-damage identity. The team acknowledged the current 5-stack-to-proc Maelstrom Weapon doesn't feel good below full stacks and is still discussing fixes.

### Hunter
- **Stable slots will increase** over time, with multiple unlock methods planned — starting conservative in the current beta build.
- **Melee weaving on survival is intentional**, but auto-shot timing is currently bugged (a fix is in progress; expect it to still be rough this week, including the 0.5-second wand wind-up). Two intended playstyles: stay in melee the whole fight (using Mongoose Bite, Raptor Strike, Strider Kick, traps), or weave between ranged and melee bursts.
- Raptor Strike was deliberately kept as a non-instant ability placed deep in the Survival tree — in early testing it was instant, and Marksmanship hunters combining it with ranged burst was far too strong.
- **The dead zone is staying.** The designers were explicit: it's a core strengths-and-weaknesses tradeoff — hunters excel at range and are vulnerable up close, and that's intentional. If you don't like it, survival is built to mitigate it.

### Warlock
- **Destruction is deliberately a Fire/Shadow hybrid**, not "a worse fire mage" — the two schools are meant to be mixed together for maximum damage, not treated as alternate specs.
- **Fear's range nerf in the affliction talent tree was an unintentional bug**, not a design choice — the old talent boosted "all affliction spells" by 20% range, and the new version only boosts *damaging* spells, inadvertently dropping Fear. Confirmed it needs a fix; exact solution (base 30-yard Fear vs. a separate range talent) is still undecided.
- **SL (Soul Link/Sacrifice) staying dominant in 1v1 PvP is considered acceptable** — the team doesn't balance around 1v1 and is fine with a build that spans two talent trees being PvP-specialized, so long as any Warlock build remains viable in a battleground overall.
- A new talent that shares Life Tap's mana with your pet made the **Imp noticeably stronger** in testing — strong enough the imp needed a damage nerf after out-damaging a demonology warlock's own pet in one test.
- **Master Demonologist was reworked**: its buffs now deliberately mirror-oppose Demonic Sacrifice's buffs, so sacrificing one pet and summoning a different one (available via the new 31-point talent) lets you stack both sets of buffs together instead of the two talents competing for the same gameplay space.

### Mage
- The **Blizzard (AoE spell) nerf, and the matching trim to Cone of Cold's duration**, both specifically target "dungeon kiting" farming — frost mages pulling and kiting entire dungeons solo, which the team called a major driver of gold-bot farming and economy damage. The intent isn't to gut mage raid AoE, just to guarantee a window where a kited pack isn't permanently snared.
- **Ice Block baseline is not happening right now** — it was considered to help non-frost PvP builds compete, but the team feels it would dilute frost's core fantasy; not ruled out forever, just "no plans at this time."
- The team acknowledged mage, especially arcane and frost, currently feel short on buttons and said they're actively looking at adding more over the next few weeks.

### Priest
- Shadow priest is designed to bring real damage first, with utility layered on top (not a repeat of the old "mana battery" raid-utility role) — including self-healing via vampiric abilities and a specced-into silence, but deliberately no party-wide mana return.
- The tier set bonuses currently visible on the PTR/beta aren't final — they were designed roughly eight months ago, before a lot of the current class changes, and the team plans a full pass before launch.
- **Smite ("Holy DPS") priest is an intentional build** — in fact it was found to be *overperforming*, specifically out-damaging some dedicated DPS specs when buffed by Judgement of the Crusader, and has since been nerfed down. Because it retains full healing capability, it's paying one of the largest hybrid-tax penalties in the game.
- **Holy Fire's 3.5-second cast time might come down**, possibly toward Wrath's 2-second version — "there's a chance," though nothing is confirmed.
- **Discipline vs. Holy**: Discipline leans into shields (uniquely strong, priest-only) and single-target tank healing; Holy leans into whole-raid healing. Discipline also got an indirect buff once the warrior rage-from-damage-taken bug is fixed, since Power Word: Shield blocks rage generation from damage taken — tanks wearing a disc shield will generate less rage, a tradeoff the team flagged warriors should be aware of.
