With Classic Plus officially rolling out in 2026, the community’s expectations were naturally tempered by Blizzard’s historical track record. Honestly, my expectations were low too. But looking at what’s actually made it into the game, I’m pleasantly surprised. Sure, not everything is a home run, but Blizzard has introduced a suite of additions—ranging from smart technical toggles to controversial race additions—that manage to shake things up without completely breaking the soul of the game. 

Let's dive into a breakdown of the biggest additions hitting our **WoW Forever** guides and blog this patch cycle.

---

## 1. The Skyborne Race & Druid Forms: Controversial, But It Is What It Is

The introduction of the brand-new **Skyborne** race has easily been the most divisive topic in the community. 

* **Lore Accuracy:** Let's be real—while they fit into the broader narrative expansion, many of us feel Blizzard could have introduced a much cooler, more lore-accurate race from the archives. 
* **The Verdict:** It is what it is, and honestly, I don't care all that much. They play fine, but they definitely lean away from traditional Classic roots.
* **Druid Forms:** For those rolling the new race as Druids, the specialized forms—like the avian-themed flight, bear, and cat variants—bring a unique aesthetic to the table, even if they take some getting used to next to classic models.

## 2. The Undead Paladin Mount

On a much brighter thematic note, the new **Undead Paladin mount** is an absolute visual treat. For players who’ve wanted a darker, more gothic aesthetic for holy warriors roaming the plague-ridden lands, this skeletal, armored steed fits the eerie atmosphere of Lordaeron to a T. It hits that sweet spot of looking cool and menacing without feeling completely out of place.

## 3. Savior Features: Toggleable HD/SD, Animations, and Transmog

This is arguably the best decision Blizzard made for the longevity of Classic Plus. Knowing how split the player base is between purists and modern QoL fans, implementing **toggleable HD/SD graphics with updated animations** alongside **toggleable transmog** is a masterstroke. 

* **Keeping Everyone Happy:** If you want the gritty, low-poly 2004 look, you can keep it toggled off. If you want cleaner animations and crisp HD assets, you flip the switch. 
* **Transmog:** Same goes for transmog—having it toggleable prevents the game from turning into a chaotic fashion show for those who want strict visual identity cohesion while letting others customize their look. This single feature keeps the peace.

---

## 4. Environmental Effects & Immersion

Instead of rewriting the game world or changing core models, the new **environmental effects** enhance what’s already there. Roaming through a genuinely foggy Duskwood or dealing with dynamic weather patterns adds a layer of depth and immersiveness that makes the old world feel alive again. It respects the original zones while drastically improving the atmospheric storytelling.

## 5. The AI Rainbow Unicorn Meme & Store Mount Panic

The internet loves a good panic. Recently, an AI-generated image of a ridiculous **"Swift Empyrean Galestrider" rainbow unicorn mount** made the rounds (complete with chat streams reacting to it), sparking mass community outrage that Blizzard was instantly filling Classic Plus with store-bought absurdity. 

<GuideImage alt="A rainbow-maned unicorn mount named Swift Empyrean Galestrider shown in-game and in a mounts tooltip, from a Twitch stream (webcam corner redacted)" src="/images/blog/classic-plus-new/rainbow-unicorn-mount.webp" />

While it turned out to be a community-generated meme designed to poke fun at modern microtransactions, it highlights how fragile trust is. It also emphasizes why features like toggleable transmog and strict visual boundaries matter so much to the player base right now.

---

## 6. Talents, Classes, and the Slippery Slope

* **Talent Overhauls:** The talent changes have been fantastic. Rather than reinventing the wheel, modifying existing trees has made multiple specs actually viable instead of locking players into a single cookie-cutter build per class.
* **Class Design:** Blizzard has kept new class abilities minimal, which is a massive win. I hope they continue to **start slow and update/make changes quickly** as data rolls in.
* **The One Worry:** My biggest concern remains the **slippery slope of adding entirely new classes down the line**. I simply don't trust the implementation balance long-term, so keeping the focus on enhancing existing classes is the right play.
